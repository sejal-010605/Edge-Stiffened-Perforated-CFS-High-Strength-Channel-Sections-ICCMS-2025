## Overview

This research investigates the structural behaviour and design of 
edge-stiffened, perforated cold-formed high-strength steel (CFS-HSS) 
channel columns subjected to axial compression.

Cold-formed steel channel sections are widely used as lightweight 
structural members because of their high strength-to-weight ratio and 
efficient material utilisation. Web perforations are frequently introduced 
to accommodate building services and reduce structural weight. However, 
these openings disturb the stress distribution of the section and can 
significantly influence its buckling behaviour and axial load-carrying 
capacity.

The research focuses on the use of edge stiffeners around web perforations 
to improve the structural performance of perforated high-strength steel 
channel sections. Numerical modelling and parametric analysis are used to 
investigate their behaviour and assess the applicability of existing 
design provisions.

---

## Research Objectives

The main objectives of this work were to:

- Investigate the axial compression behaviour of edge-stiffened perforated 
  cold-formed high-strength steel channel sections.
- Develop finite element models capable of capturing the nonlinear 
  behaviour and buckling response of the columns.
- Study the influence of web perforations and edge stiffening on the 
  ultimate axial capacity.
- Investigate the behaviour of sections over a range of member geometries 
  and lengths.
- Examine the interaction between local, distortional, and global buckling 
  behaviour.
- Compare numerical predictions with existing design approaches.
- Assess the applicability of current international design provisions to 
  edge-stiffened perforated high-strength steel sections.

---

## Structural Configuration

The investigated members consist of cold-formed high-strength steel channel 
sections containing web perforations.

Unlike conventional unstiffened openings, the perforations considered in 
this study incorporate edge stiffening around the opening. The stiffened 
perforation modifies the local stiffness and stress distribution around the 
opening and consequently influences the buckling response of the member.

The structural behaviour is therefore governed by the interaction between:

- cross-sectional geometry,
- web perforation geometry,
- perforation edge stiffening,
- material strength,
- member slenderness, and
- local, distortional, and global buckling modes.

## Numerical Modelling

Finite element analysis was used to investigate the nonlinear structural 
response of the columns.

The numerical modelling framework included:

### Geometry Modelling

Detailed models of the cold-formed channel sections were developed while 
accounting for the section geometry and edge-stiffened web perforations.

### Material Modelling

The nonlinear stress-strain behaviour of high-strength steel was 
incorporated into the finite element models to represent material 
nonlinearity.

### Finite Element Discretisation

Shell finite elements were employed to model the thin-walled steel 
sections, enabling the numerical models to capture local plate deformation 
and member-level buckling behaviour.

### Boundary Conditions and Loading

The columns were subjected to axial compression using appropriate end 
conditions and loading arrangements to reproduce the intended structural 
response.

### Geometric Imperfections

Initial geometric imperfections were incorporated into the numerical 
models because the behaviour of thin-walled cold-formed steel members is 
highly sensitive to geometric imperfections.

### Nonlinear Analysis

Geometric and material nonlinearities were considered to determine the 
ultimate axial load and post-buckling response of the members.

The validation process considered quantities such as:

Following validation, the modelling methodology was used for the broader

## Parametric Investigation

A systematic parametric investigation was conducted to study the influence 
of key geometric and structural parameters.

The numerical programme examined the response of multiple section 
configurations and member lengths, allowing the effects of slenderness, 
perforations, and edge stiffening to be evaluated.

The study focused particularly on changes in:

- ultimate axial load capacity,
- elastic and inelastic buckling response,
- local buckling behaviour,
- distortional buckling behaviour,
- global buckling behaviour, and
- interaction between different instability modes.

---

## Buckling Behaviour

Cold-formed steel members are particularly susceptible to instability 
because of their thin-walled geometry.

Three principal instability mechanisms were considered in the numerical 
investigation:

### Local Buckling

Local buckling involves deformation of individual plate elements forming 
the cross-section while the overall shape of the section remains 
approximately unchanged.

### Distortional Buckling

Distortional buckling involves deformation of the cross-section accompanied 
by rotation or displacement of the flange-lip assembly.

### Global Buckling

Global buckling corresponds to instability of the complete member and 
becomes increasingly important for longer and more slender columns.

The presence of web perforations and their associated edge stiffeners can 
alter the relative contribution and interaction of these buckling modes.

---

## Design Assessment

The numerical results were compared with existing design approaches for 
cold-formed steel members.

The investigation was used to evaluate whether current design provisions 
adequately represent the axial capacity of edge-stiffened perforated 
high-strength steel channel sections.

The study highlights the importance of accounting for the combined effects 
of:

- high-strength steel,
- web perforations,
- perforation edge stiffening, and
- buckling-mode interaction

when predicting the resistance of these members.

## Abstract

Cold-formed high-strength steel channel sections offer significant 
structural efficiency due to their high strength-to-weight ratio. Web 
perforations are commonly required in practical construction applications 
but can adversely affect the buckling behaviour and load-carrying capacity 
of thin-walled members.

This research investigates the axial compression behaviour of perforated 
cold-formed high-strength steel channel columns incorporating edge 
stiffening around the web openings. Validated finite element models are 
used to examine their nonlinear response and investigate the effects of 
section geometry, perforations, member slenderness, and buckling behaviour.

The numerical results are subsequently used to assess the applicability of 
existing design approaches to these advanced cold-formed steel sections.
